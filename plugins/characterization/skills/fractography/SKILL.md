title: Determine Failure Mode and Initiation Site From a Fracture Surface
description: Read a fracture surface systematically for initiation site, crack-growth features, and final-fracture region before attributing a cause.
keywords:
- level:L1

---

A fracture surface reads at multiple scales, low magnification first: the
initiation site (often at a surface, near-surface defect, or internal
inclusion/pore), the crack-growth region, and the final overload/fast-fracture
region, and their relative extent. A large fatigue-growth region with a
small overload region indicates a low applied stress relative to the
material's toughness, and vice versa.

Features indicate mechanism: fatigue striations and beach marks (progressive
cyclic growth), dimpled/ductile rupture (final overload in a ductile
material), cleavage or intergranular facets (brittle or
environmentally-assisted fracture). A mechanism rests on the features
actually observed; the loading history alone only suggests one.

The initiation site often connects to a specific feature: a pore or
inclusion visible at the origin (cross-reference `xct` if available), a
surface roughness feature or machining mark, or a design stress
concentration. A fractographic finding that identifies WHERE and often WHAT
initiated failure is far stronger evidence than a fatigue-life number alone.
