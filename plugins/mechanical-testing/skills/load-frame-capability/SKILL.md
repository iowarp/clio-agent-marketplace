title: Check What a Load Frame, Load Cell and Extensometer Can Resolve
description: Use before trusting a mechanical test result at a required precision, to check what the load frame, load cell and strain measurement can resolve.
keywords:
- level:L1

---

The load frame type (servo-hydraulic vs. electromechanical) and its control
system determine achievable loading frequency range and dynamic response,
which matters for fatigue testing at higher frequencies or for capturing fast
transients.

Load cell capacity and resolution relate to the actual loads in the test: a
load cell rated far above the test load loses resolution at the low end,
while one too close to capacity risks overload and reduces safety margin. An
out-of-calibration load cell invalidates the absolute accuracy of every
reported load.

Extensometer/strain-measurement range and resolution relate to the expected
strain in the test, and gripping/alignment can introduce bending that biases
strain readings: misalignment is a common, often-undiagnosed source of
scatter in tensile and fatigue data.

Capacities, resolutions and calibration dates come from the specific system's
own specification sheets and calibration certificates, which the scientist or
the lab supplies; the vendor or model name does not give them.
