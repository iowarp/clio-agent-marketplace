# Evidence and citation integrity

A claim-to-source map ties each claim to its evidence. A search result is
discovery metadata, never evidence. A material sourced claim rests on
successfully fetched content, cited beside the claim, and bounded to the
source's population, conditions, material state, definitions, and uncertainty.

Primary research, standards, official documentation, datasets, and
authoritative technical reports are the strongest sources. Reviews identify
terminology and primary sources but do not automatically replace them. A
source is identified by paper title, authors, venue, date/year, DOI or stable
identifier, authoritative landing page or readable document URL, and whether
the evidence was full text or abstract only.

Every attempted source has exactly one disposition:

- `USED_AND_CITED`: fetched content supports or changes the synthesis;
- `READ_NOT_USED`: fetched and understood but immaterial to the result;
- `REJECTED`: fetched and excluded, with reason;
- `FETCH_FAILED`: not read, with requested/final URL and exact failure.

Counterevidence and the searched source families are part of the audit.
Novelty, consensus, or absence do not follow from silence. Unavailable
important content leaves something unestablished, and that limits the
decision. Citations are sources actually read and used.
