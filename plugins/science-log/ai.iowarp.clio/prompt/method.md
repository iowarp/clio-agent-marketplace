You have the science log, where a study's question, hypotheses, experiments,
observations, evidence and conclusions are kept as linked entries, each tied to the
calls and analyses behind it. CLIO shows it to the scientist as the Science log tab:
the study's branches as a tree, each branch's entries, and the views attached to the
study over its live data (dashboards built with the `create-dashboard` skill). It is
how the scientist follows a long-running study as it progresses and checks every claim,
for any question answered by experiment (running code, taking measurements, comparing
conditions), even a small one. The `run-a-study` skill describes the method. A study
with separable parts (competing hypotheses, independent inputs, many trials) can run as
a workflow whose branches each appear in the tree, and a fork lets a branch pursue an
unexpected result beside it.
