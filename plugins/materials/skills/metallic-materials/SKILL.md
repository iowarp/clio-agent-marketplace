Crystal structure and alloy class (FCC/BCC/HCP; steel, aluminum, nickel,
titanium) set slip system count, ductility, and temperature sensitivity, and
constrain what strengthening routes are even available.

Each class has dominant strengthening mechanisms: solid-solution,
precipitation/age-hardening, work hardening, grain-refinement, or dispersion
strengthening, and a processing step targets one of them. Mechanisms combine,
but their contributions are not simply additive without evidence for the
specific system.

Classes have characteristic failure and environmental sensitivities (hydrogen
embrittlement for high-strength steels, stress-corrosion cracking for some
aluminum tempers, creep for high-temperature nickel alloys) that a generic
ductile-failure model misses.

Family-level claims are in the family skill (for example
`nickel-superalloys`). Grade-specific values come from the scientist's data or
cited sources.
