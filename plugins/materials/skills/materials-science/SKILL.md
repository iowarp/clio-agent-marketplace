The chain runs from processing history (composition, thermal path,
deformation, cooling rate, atmosphere) to microstructure (phases and
fractions, grain size and morphology, texture, precipitates, porosity,
residual stress) to properties (elastic, yield and flow, toughness, fatigue,
creep, transport) to in-service performance.

Each link is a claim needing its own support. A processing change that is
expected to refine grain size still requires evidence that it did, and that
the refinement moved the property that matters. Each pair connects through a
mechanism that holds under some conditions.

A linkage does not transport across material classes, length scales, or
property regimes without justification: a Hall-Petch slope, a precipitate
strengthening increment, or a texture effect is specific to its system. Once
the alloy class or family is known, the more specific skill
(`metallic-materials`, `nickel-superalloys`) applies; grade-specific values
come from the scientist's data or cited sources. The weakest link is where
characterization or simulation helps most.
