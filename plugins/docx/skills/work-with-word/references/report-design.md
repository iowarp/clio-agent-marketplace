# Report design

## The report before formatting

The supplied material shows who will use the report, what question it answers
and which claims the sources support. A concise opening summary carries the
principal finding, its confidence and the next decision. Enough context and
method to understand the evidence follow, then findings in a useful order,
limitations and next steps. An operational report and a scientific report do not
need identical sections.

A report explains relationships and implications in connected paragraphs.
Bullets suit genuinely parallel findings or actions. A heading followed only by
one bullet is often an outline rather than developed content. Length follows the
evidence and intended use; generic padding and a substantial analysis collapsed
into a one-page fixture both misrepresent it.

Reported facts, interpretation and proposed work stay distinguishable. Figures
and numbers carry where they came from, their units, exclusions and uncertainty.
An exploratory association is not a causal conclusion.

## Document structure carries the design

Page size and margins are explicit settings. 11–12 pt body text, a readable font
available on the execution host, and consistent paragraph spacing are a sound
base. Native Title, Heading 1 and Heading 2 styles are real headings; manually
enlarged paragraphs only look like them. A small type hierarchy, one restrained
accent colour and aligned left edges hold a report together. An internal report
usually does not need a mostly empty cover page.

An existing report's actual heading and table styles are the ones to reuse. A
document from another authoring library may have custom names rather than the
built-in Word styles, visible in its styles and a nearby section. Bold body text
or an unrelated table style substituted to avoid a missing-style error breaks
the document's structure. The rendered overview shows whether an inserted
heading, explanation and table landed in the intended order.

Section breaks change orientation or headers. A multi-page document has page
numbers. Headings kept with the following paragraph, widow control and captions
beside their figures prevent stranded lines. A deliberate page break helps a
specific page; blank paragraphs are not a spacing system.

## Tables and figures are evidence

Native tables stay editable. Meaningful column headings, units and explicit
widths that fit the page make them readable, and a continued table repeats its
header row. A very wide or long table can split, take a landscape section or
move its full detail to an appendix; shrinking everything leaves unreadable text.

Images keep their proportions. The highest useful source resolution reads best;
a thumbnail stretched into a page-wide figure does not. Labels are judged at the
size the reader will actually see. A crop or separate panels help only when the
evidence survives and becomes easier to read. A caption states what the figure
shows and any material caveat, with a source reference. Decorative illustrations
in place of charts, or body text flattened into an image, lose the evidence.

## Page review

Reopening the DOCX shows structure and retained objects. The rendered page
overviews, including the last page's flow, show heading orphans, unexpected blank
pages, table splits, clipped text and misplaced images. Individual pages resolve
what the overview leaves unclear, such as small labels or headers/footers at
overview scale; not every page needs opening individually. Paragraph quality
matters as well as overflow. An old PDF cannot verify a new DOCX; a revision of
the native source shows in its regenerated preview.

The editable source publishes with its version-bound preview. A PDF is useful
for reading, but does not replace the requested Word document.
