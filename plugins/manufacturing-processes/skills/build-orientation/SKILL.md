Build orientation is relative to a defined reference (build-plate normal /
Z-axis) for a feature or specimen; "the part" does not have one orientation if
different regions or specimens were built at different angles.

Orientation has three characteristic consequences: crystallographic texture
and columnar grain growth generally align with the local heat-flow direction
(often close to build direction), producing directional strength and fatigue
behavior; down-facing and shallow-angle surfaces require support structures
and exhibit rougher as-built surface finish than up-facing or vertical
surfaces; and staircasing on curved/angled surfaces sets an
orientation-dependent achievable surface accuracy.

Properties measured on a vertically-built specimen do not carry over to a
horizontally-built feature of the same part, or vice versa: a property claim
covers the orientation it was measured at.
